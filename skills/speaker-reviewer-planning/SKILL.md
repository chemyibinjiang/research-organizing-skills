# Speaker and Reviewer Planning

## Establish the Decision

Separate speaker discovery, invitation, reviewer recruitment, assignment and award
selection. Identify the organizer approving each step. Confirm the event's own
names (for example Student Forum and Poster Night), selection rules, capacities
and deadlines; do not copy a previous conference's panel size or awards quota.

## Find and Compare Candidates

- Translate the session themes or abstract topics into a short expertise map.
  For new candidates, use current institutional pages and relevant publications;
  retain the source and why the work fits. Do not invent credentials or availability.
- Produce an explainable shortlist with topic fit, current affiliation, career
  stage when verified, invitation status, availability and contact route. If a
  guest uses an assistant, preserve that route in subsequent correspondence.
- Assess breadth across research approaches, institutions, regions and career
  stages using known facts. Institution country is not nationality. Do not infer
  gender, ethnicity or nationality from names/photos or use them to rank quality.
  Explain the denominator, missing values and data source in diversity summaries.
- Record invited, accepted, declined and no response separately. A candidate is
  not a confirmed chair or judge merely because their name appears in a plan.

## Match Reviewers

1. Map abstracts to topics and reviewer expertise with brief reasons. Read enough
   of each abstract to distinguish a keyword match from substantive fit.
2. Ask organizers/reviewers about conflicts: authorship, supervision, collaboration
   and other event-defined exclusions. Absence from the declared-conflict list is
   not a completed conflict check. Avoid same-institution assignments when the
   event requires it; do not silently invent a universal policy.
3. Balance coverage and workload, then obtain organizer approval. Keep reviewer
   acceptance, assessment availability and session attendance as separate facts.
4. For group judging, give every group member the same complete group list. Define
   who returns the group result, how many selections, whether ranking is needed,
   the deadline, and how absent presenters and ties are handled.

Use [the validator schema](references/assignment-schema.md) and
`scripts/check_assignments.py` for deterministic checks. It does not choose
scientific winners, assess expertise or establish fairness.

## Correct Names and Handle Late Changes

Keep stable abstract/person/poster IDs distinct from display names. Compare the
requested fields against the original abstract and later explicit corrections.
Do not reverse names or expand initials without evidence. A request to verify
names does not authorize overwriting titles that authors later revised by email.

After publication/printing, retain withdrawn slots and a change log. A replacement
using an old display number needs explicit approval, its own submission ID and a
mapping to the former occupant; never silently reuse an identity or renumber all
posters. Treat display-only posters as separate from award eligibility. Reconcile
restored attendance against later withdrawals before sending a batch.

## Deliver and Close

Return a proposed roster, rationale, conflicts/unknowns, workload summary and
validation findings. For execution, prepare group instructions, collection time,
result forms and a small change sheet for already printed packets. Use the mail
workflow for approved notifications; editing a list is not notification.
Archive results, reconcile names and confirm recipients before award emails or
finance handoff. Keep bank and identity information out of reviewer packets.
